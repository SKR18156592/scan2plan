# scan2plan — technical report

*Applied AI Engineer case study, Round 2. ≤ 6 pages. Numbers in this report are
regenerated by `scripts/run_benchmark.sh` (LiDAR tier, repeatability, drift
ablation, schema) and the scripts named in each section.*

## 1. What was built, and what the data allowed

One command per capture turns a phone capture into `plan.json` (schema in
`schema/plan.schema.json`) and a rendered `plan.png`: per-room walls, floor
area, ceiling height, openings, a stitched multi-room plan with adjacency,
damage regions on surfaces, concealed-damage flags with the rule that fired,
scope line items keyed to surface ids, and a 95% interval on every number.

The evaluation data is the three Stray Scanner captures sent with the brief:
`single_room` (37 s), `single_scan_floor_only` (115 s, whole apartment,
camera pointed down) and `single_scan_with_ceiling` (215 s, same apartment,
ceiling swept, several loops). There is **no tape/laser ground truth, no
staged damage and no consumer-app export** for them. Consequences, stated
once here and carried through every table:

- LiDAR-tier accuracy is assessed by **repeatability** (the two apartment
  captures) and internal consistency, not against tape.
- Photo/video tiers are fed from the same walk (frames of `rgb.mp4`) and
  scored **against the LiDAR plan** as reference.
- Damage detection is verified for false positives only.
- The head-to-head (Part 3) is not done: it needs a consumer-app scan of the
  same rooms, which cannot be produced from the sample.

## 2. Architecture

```
            ┌──────── LiDAR ────────┐  ┌──────────── video / photos ─────────────┐
 capture →  │ Stray: depth+conf+ARKit│  │ keyframes → DISK+LightGlue → poses      │
            │ drift: loop closure +  │  │  video: COLMAP SfM, sub-models chained  │
            │ Manhattan heading graph│  │  photos: depth graph (sim3 RANSAC)      │
            └──────────┬─────────────┘  │ depth: Depth Anything V2 Metric-Indoor  │
                       │                │  bias-calibrated, per-image scale fixed │
                       │                └───────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                       └──────► fused point cloud (y up, metric) ◄─┘
                                      │  analyze() — identical for all tiers
          floor/ceiling planes → axis-aligned wall map → rooms (door-gap bridging,
          or one room per photo folder) → global wall planes → room outlines
          snapped to planes → walls, openings, heights → errors.Profile → JSON
                                      │
          damage: OWLv2 on upright keyframes → back-project → surface + extent
                  → multi-view merge → rules CD-01..04 → scope items
```

Design choices worth defending:

- **One analysis path for all tiers.** Tiers differ only in how poses and
  depth are obtained and in their error profile. A fix to room logic helps
  every tier, and the walk-in tier choice cannot hit untested code.
- **Measure on raw points, not on the grid.** Rooms are found on a 5 cm
  occupancy grid, but every reported wall position is a plane fitted to the
  raw points (global wall planes, `walls.py`), so the grid never limits
  accuracy.
- **Walls are tall, furniture is short.** A grid cell is wall if its points
  cover ≥ 1 m of height in consecutive 10 cm slices. Using min/max height
  span instead failed: LiDAR "flying pixels" at depth edges pile up in
  mid-air on repeated passes and faked walls across the hallway.
- **Rooms = free space after bridging door gaps.** Gaps ≤ 1.2 m between
  *collinear* wall pieces are closed; the remaining free-space components are
  rooms; the bridged cells are the doorways. A distance-transform watershed
  was tried first and failed (rooms leak through wall gaps, not narrow
  passages); an unconstrained closing joined parallel walls across corridors.

## 3. Tier design and device matrix

| | Photos | Video | LiDAR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phones | any iPhone 15+ | any iPhone 15+ | Pro models (12 Pro+) |
| App | Camera | Camera | Stray Scanner (free) |
| Poses | depth graph | COLMAP + depth chaining | ARKit + drift correction |
| Depth / scale | mono, calibrated | mono, calibrated | LiDAR |
| 1σ length model | 15 cm + 50% | 10 cm + 45% | 1 cm/surface + 0.5% |
| Measured (sample) | see §6 | see §6 | see §6 |

Full matrix: `docs/DEVICE_MATRIX.md`. Capture route: Route 2, stock apps,
`docs/CAPTURE_PROTOCOL.md`.

**Mono depth, measured.** Depth Anything V2 Metric-Indoor (Base) against
LiDAR depth on the same frames (`scripts/eval_mono_depth.py`): after a
per-frame scale, relative error is 3.4–3.7% — the *shape* is good. The
*scale* is not: the network over-estimates depth by 1.341 / 1.286 / 1.392 on
the three captures. Two captures fit the correction (1.313), the third is
held out (+6% residual). Two bugs found on the way: Stray stores portrait
captures sideways, so frames reached the network rotated 90° (per-frame
scale spread p10–p90 shrank from 0.78–1.92 to 1.13–1.71 once frames were
turned upright using SfM gravity); and gravity itself was first estimated
assuming landscape (camera x level), which is wrong for portrait capture.

**Poses without LiDAR.** SIFT finds ~470 keypoints per frame on the sample's
white walls; COLMAP fragmented the 37 s clip into 7–10 models. DISK +
LightGlue (kornia) doubled the largest model. Remaining COLMAP sub-models are
chained by depth-lifted matches (3D–3D RANSAC, ≥ 40 inliers; a 12-inlier
threshold produced false links that scrambled the map). For photos, COLMAP
verified most pairs as degenerate (photos taken while turning on the spot have
no baseline) and registered 3/40; the depth graph (`mono/depthgraph.py`) needs
no baseline: per-pair similarity by RANSAC on depth-lifted matches, maximum
spanning tree, joint refinement. It placed 27/40 photos and all five room
folders in one component.

## 4. Drift handling

ARKit is gravity-aligned, so roll/pitch do not drift; yaw and horizontal
position do. `drift.py`:

1. Cut the trajectory into ~2 s fragments; fuse each (locally accurate).
2. **Loop closure:** fragments within 1.5 m of each other, ≥ 8 fragments apart
   in time, are aligned by point-to-plane ICP on **wall points only**, solving
   (x, z, yaw) with height anchored to the floor plane. An edge is rejected if
   fitness < 0.35 or the translation block is ill-conditioned (corridor).
3. **Plane-anchored heading:** each fragment's dominant wall direction (mod
   90°) is a heading measurement; in a rectilinear building they should agree.
4. Pose graph over per-fragment corrections with odometry, loop and heading
   residuals (whitened by stated sigmas, Huber loss); corrections interpolated
   to every frame.

The first version (4-DOF, all points) *reduced* loop residuals but made walls
thicker and the floor worse (with_ceiling floor residual 1.97 → 3.05 cm):
ICP was sliding fragments vertically and along corridors. Steps 2's
constraints fixed that. Step 3 was added after the repeatability analysis
found ~5° of heading drift across `single_scan_floor_only`, which has only 2
usable loop closures.

Ablation (`out/bench/ablation_*.json`; "off" = raw ARKit poses):

| | floor_only off | floor_only on | with_ceiling off | with_ceiling on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| loop closures used | – | 2 | – | 41 |
| loop residual, mean | 1.8 cm | 1.2 cm | 10.6 cm | 2.5 cm |
| heading spread across fragments | 1.80° | 0.85° | 0.79° | 0.57° |
| wall thickness (12 strongest planes) | 3.97 cm | 3.56 cm | 4.53 cm | 4.37 cm |
| floor residual | 1.92 cm | 1.93 cm | 1.97 cm | 1.97 cm |
| stitched footprint | 59.6 m² (7 rooms) | 52.6 m² (6) | 55.1 m² (6) | 58.2 m² (8) |

Correction is on by default. Every consistency metric improves or holds. The
footprint changes in both directions because drift changes which wall gaps
close, and therefore how the apartment is partitioned — the effect is real
and is the same failure §7 runs into.

## 5. Error budget and calibration

Every reported value carries σ from `errors.py`; the JSON publishes value ± 1.96σ.

- Wall surface position: statistical (1.25 · robust std / √n of the plane's
  points) ⊕ tier surface bias (LiDAR 1 cm).
- Wall length: both bounding surfaces ⊕ relative scale (LiDAR 0.5%).
- Ceiling height: floor and ceiling surfaces ⊕ scale.
- Opening width: two edge localisations on a 2.5 cm sampling grid ⊕ scale.
- Area: per-edge position × edge length ⊕ 2 × scale.
- Unobserved wall (no supporting points): 10 cm (LiDAR), drawn dashed.

**LiDAR priors are not fitted** (no ground truth). They are checked
indirectly: repeatability errors (§6) are far larger than the intervals, which
means the intervals are honest for *surface position* but do not include the
dominant error source — which room boundary a capture finds. That is stated
as the main open calibration issue.

**Mono tiers are fitted.** With first-pass priors (3–5 cm, 3–5%), interval
coverage against the LiDAR reference was **0%** (|z| ≈ 25–34) — confident
garbage. The relative sigma was then set from the measured errors (video
45%, photo 50%), giving coverage video 83%, photo 33% (from 0% for both). Footprints from mono tiers
are flagged as lower bounds when images were not placed.

## 7. The fix loop

Declaration (written before the fix, `docs/FIX_DECLARATION.md`, tag
`fixloop-before`): worst gate **repeatability**, 0/8 walls passing, median
difference 17.5 cm between the two captures of the same apartment.
Hypothesis: wall lengths are taken between vertices of a polygon traced from
the free-space mask, whose extent depends on what each capture saw; the wall
*surfaces* agree. Fix: detect wall planes globally and snap room outlines to
them. Prediction: median ≤ 3 cm, pass rate 30–50%.

| (harness v2: rotation locked to k·90°) | before (`fixloop-before`) | after (`main`) |
|---|---|---|
| walls passing | 0 / 9 | 0 / 12 |
| median difference | 16.0 cm | 12.9 cm |
| p90 difference | 97.3 cm | 29.0 cm |
| rooms matched | 4 | 5 |
| gate | fail | fail |

Regenerate: `out/before` and `out/bench` via §9.

**Post-mortem.** The prediction was badly wrong. The shipped fix moved the
number, but evaluating it exposed two things the declaration missed:

1. *The scoring harness let rotation float.* Both plans are in their
   dominant-wall frames, so the true relative rotation is a multiple of 90°;
   free rotation let mismatched rooms tilt the registration. Locking it
   changed the before-number from 17.5 to 16.0 cm (both reported).
2. *A second root cause, heading drift.* With rotation locked, the plans
   disagreed by ~1° globally. Per-fragment wall direction over time showed
   ~5° of yaw drift in `single_scan_floor_only` that its two loop closures
   could not remove. The Manhattan heading prior (§4) halved the heading
   spread (1.80° → 0.85°) and is part of the shipped after-run.

What remains is the hypothesis's own target, only partly addressed: the two
captures still partition the apartment differently (a room merged with the
hall in one, split in the other), and an outline edge snaps to the first
plane it meets, which is sometimes a wardrobe front. The gate (1 cm) is
tighter than two LiDAR surface biases; even perfect partitioning would leave
it marginal. The next fix is to build rooms from the plane arrangement
itself (cells bounded by wall planes) rather than from free-space masks.

## 8. Known failure modes

- **Partitioning.** Wide openings (> 1.2 m) merge rooms; tall furniture
  (wardrobes, fridges) is taken as wall; a staircase in the sample apartment
  violates the single-floor assumption.
- **Mirrors and glass.** LiDAR returns the reflected room behind a mirror and
  nothing from glass; mirrored geometry appears as a phantom room beyond the
  wall. Mitigation: room components need observed floor and the camera to be
  near them; the protocol says not to dwell on mirrors/glass. Not benchmarked.
- **Wet/glossy floors.** Specular floors drop LiDAR returns (low confidence,
  filtered); the floor plane then comes from fewer points and the σ widens.
- **Low light.** ARKit tracking and SfM degrade; protocol requires lights on.
- **Mono tiers.** On the sample (close-range, blurry, white walls) poses are
  incomplete and geometry poor; intervals were calibrated to say so.
- **Damage.** Zero-shot detector, no damage-domain training; true-positive
  rate unmeasured. Multi-view consistency is the main false-positive filter
  (LiDAR/video); a single photo can produce a false region.
- **Memory.** Mono tiers on an 8 GB machine swap; the photo run on 40 images
  took ~40 min wall-clock at 3% CPU. 16 GB is recommended.
